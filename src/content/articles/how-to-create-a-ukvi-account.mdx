You need a UKVI account to see your eVisa, prove your right to work or rent, and travel in and out of the UK. Setting one up is free and done online. This guide gives you the exact steps, the documents you need, and how to fix the most common problems. If you are still unsure what an eVisa is, read our [guide to the UK eVisa](/articles/what-is-evisa-uk) first.

## What you need to create a UKVI account

You need three things to start:

- An email address you can use each time you sign in
- A phone number you can use each time you sign in
- One identity document from the list below

For the identity document, you need **one** of these:

- A valid passport and your visa application number (GWF or UAN)
- A valid passport and your BRP number
- An expired BRP card (you can still use it until 31 December 2026)

You also need a smartphone to confirm your identity with an app. There is another way to do this if you do not have a passport or a smartphone.

## How to create your UKVI account step by step

Follow these steps in order:

1. Go to the GOV.UK page "get access to your eVisa".
2. Enter the email address and phone number you want to use.
3. Enter one identity document — a passport with an application number, a passport with a BRP number, or an expired BRP.
4. Download the "UK Immigration: ID Check" app on your phone.
5. Use the app to scan your document and your face.
6. Finish creating the account and link it to your eVisa.

Once your account is linked, you can sign in any time to view your eVisa. Setting it up costs nothing.

## How to access and prove your eVisa

Sign in to your UKVI account to view your eVisa online. To prove your status to an employer, landlord, or other service, you generate a **share code**.

A share code is a set of letters and numbers. You give it to the person who needs to check your status. Each share code is valid for 90 days. You can generate a new one any time.

For travel, add your current passport to your UKVI account before you leave the UK. Border Force checks your status digitally against your passport. Your [travel rights with ILR](/articles/ilr-travel-rights-uk) do not change — but your passport must be linked.

## Who needs a UKVI account

You need a UKVI account if you have:

- A UK visa of any length
- Settlement in the UK, such as [Indefinite Leave to Remain](/articles/what-is-indefinite-leave-to-remain)
- A digital certificate showing right of abode or exemption

Partners and children who joined you as dependants each need their **own** account. You cannot hold their status inside your account. If your BRP has expired, you still need an account — the [old BRP card](/articles/biometric-residence-permit-guide) is no longer valid proof on its own.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Waiting until you need to travel**  
People leave the account setup until the week they fly. If the ID check has a problem, you can miss your flight. Set it up now, while there is no pressure.

❌ **Using an email you will lose access to**  
Some people use a work email or an old address. You need this email every time you sign in. Use a personal email you will keep for years.

❌ **Thinking the expired BRP card still proves your status**  
Most BRP cards expired on 31 December 2024. Showing the card alone is not valid proof now. You must use your eVisa and a share code instead.

❌ **Assuming one account covers the whole family**  
Each family member has their own immigration status. Each person needs their own UKVI account, including children.

## Expert Tips (What Most Guides Miss)

1. **Link every new passport straight away.** When you renew your passport, add it to your UKVI account the same day. Border Force checks your status against the passport you travel on.
2. **Generate a fresh share code each time.** Do not reuse an old code close to its 90-day limit. Generate a new one when an employer or landlord asks, so it does not expire mid-check.
3. **Screenshot your account details.** Save your UKVI account sign-in details and your BRP or passport number in a safe place. Recovery is slow if you lose them.
4. **Set it up before applying for citizenship.** You will need clear proof of your status when you [apply for British citizenship](/articles/how-to-apply-for-british-citizenship). A working UKVI account makes that step simple.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a UKVI account free to set up?

Yes. GOV.UK states you do not need to pay to set up a UKVI account or access your eVisa. Anyone asking you to pay a fee for the account itself is not official.

### What do I need to create a UKVI account?

You need an email address, a phone number, and one identity document. The document can be a valid passport with your application number, a passport with your BRP number, or an expired BRP card.

### Can I still use my BRP if it has expired?

You cannot use an expired BRP as everyday proof of status. But you can still use an expired BRP to set up your UKVI account until 31 December 2026.

### How do I prove my immigration status without a card?

You sign in to your UKVI account and generate a share code. You give this code to the employer, landlord, or service. Each share code is valid for 90 days.

### Do my children need their own UKVI accounts?

Yes. Every person with their own immigration status needs their own UKVI account. You cannot hold a child's status inside your account.

### What if I do not have a passport or a smartphone?

There is an alternative way to confirm your identity if you do not have a valid passport, an expired BRP, or access to a smartphone. GOV.UK explains this option during the setup process.
